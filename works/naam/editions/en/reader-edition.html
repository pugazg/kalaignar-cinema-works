Narrative

In a village, a puja to Mari is underway. Kumaran arrives there with his mother. Jeevanandar, one of the villagers who comes their way, meets Kumaran.

குமரன் :-Hmm... what's that celebration...?

ஜீவானந்தர் :-It's no celebration, son... We're conducting a puja for the goddess to put an end to our hardship.

கும :-A puja......!

ஜீவா :-There has been no rain in the village, so there is no harvest. With no harvest, we have no means to pay the zamindar's tax. That's why we're conducting a puja for Mariatha.

கும :-Oh... ho... sir... careful! Keep increasing the puja and a storm and rain may come together.

ஜீவா :-What can we do, son? We have to go the way the people go.

கும :-If Mari, who shows no compassion at the sight of your tears, can be bewitched by rosewater... then doesn't that mean you are deceiving her?

ஜீவா :-That's true too! What you say is true! Yes... who are you? What place are you from?

நாராயணி :(Kumaran's mother) We're from out of town. We came to see Physician Sanjeevi. His house......

ஜீவா :-Physician Sanjeevi......? He'll be at home! If not, there's a hut where he prepares poisonous herbs! He may be there! In any case, go a little that way and ask around.

கும :-We'll be going, then! (Jeevanandar leaves.)

நாரா :Son... wasn't it after talking like this there that we came to this town? Even here, why are you picking quarrels with everyone who comes and goes?

கும :-It's not a quarrel, Amma... just a small explanation about the connection between rain and Mari......!

நாரா :-All right... all right... come, let's go.

(The temple puja comes to an end.)

மலையப்பன் :-Kumara! You have laid your head on the sacrificial altar.

குமரன் :-Kill me! But, cruel man, do not think that killing a man will destroy an ideology!

மலையப்பன் :-Your idea will be smashed to a hundred pieces!

குமரன் :-You have read only one half of history! You may have gathered the bones of those smashed to pieces—written down the accounts of those who suffered and shed tears—but you have not read the chapter of revolution that rose on the foundation of those teardrops! Not because your eyesight is defective! Because your ancestral scripture has written that reading is a sin! Set fire if you will, attack treacherously if you will! Let bees sting like scorpions if they will! Let prison open its mouth and welcome us if it will! Shave our heads if you will! Commit brutality if you will! This is a rationalist principle that cannot be destroyed! Ten-and-a-half-touch gold!

மலையப்பன் :-Leave it! Otherwise this very house will become your cremation ground.

(A fight breaks out between them. The priest standing outside runs away. Kumaran carries the child and comes out. Malayappan, struck by Kumaran, collapses. This is Kumaran's last breath.)

குமரன் :-Life ... life ... for me, who brought a charter of rights, a charter of death? Look, that day you stopped these policemen from arresting me. Today, those same policemen have come to save me from you. What harm have I done to you?

(Meenu enters.)

குமரன் :-As I am about to give up my life, I ask one last help from you. You have love for me.

மீனு :-I will do anything. Kumaran!

குமரன் :-You must live.

மீனு :-I am one who cannot live, Kumaran!

குமரன் :-Not for yourself! For the children! Jeevanandar! I make my properties the property of the people! I alone am dying. But my ideas have not died. They will not die... with that satisfaction in my heart, I leave you. Long live self-respect; may rationalism triumph.

(Kumaran loses his life.)

“Is this the end for all the noble people who reform the world?”

Who will reshape this?
