Written text
Dialogue: M. Karunanidhi.
Narrative
The ruler of Kurinji country had two wives. Both were pregnant. Durjayan, the elder brother of the younger queen, had one particular desire: to preserve his power, his sister's child should rule the land. Because of that.................
கோபதிவர்மன்Murderess. Enough of your antics. After carrying out sabotage there, you dance here to cover it up.
சித்ராWhat?
கோபதிYou made a plan to ruin Singari's life—do you know that plan is dead? You wretch.
சித்ராA plan? What plan?
கோபதிAh! What acting, you wicked woman! Even throwing you into the fire and burning you to ashes would not be enough.
அன்பானந்தர்Your Majesty, anger will obscure justice. Calmly....
கோபதிCalmly, Anbanandar! You yourself question this Neeli!
அன்பாMaharani, you are jealous because the younger queen is pregnant; you fear that child may claim a share in the kingdom. Because of that, you mixed poison into the medicine sent to the younger queen.
சித்ராAh!
அன்பாThis is the younger Maharani's accusation.
சித்ராDo you believe this?
கோபதிI believe it completely. Chitra, not just Singari—you would dare even to kill me for your own selfish ends!
சித்ராAn outrageous fabrication. I am innocent! Do not tear my heart apart.
கோபதிTch! Do not touch me. (He pushes her down.)
அன்பாYour Majesty! She is fully pregnant.
கோபதிPregnant! Singari too is pregnant.
சித்ராMy lord, I regard Singari as my younger sister.
கோபதிA lie—an utter lie!
சித்ராThere is no one to plead for me. Singari has her brother on her side. And she has the king, who is dear to her. Truth alone is my witness!
அன்பாMaharaja! To clarify the matter, both the complainant and the accused must be questioned.
கோபதிA vain hope. Even so, let no one say the ruler of Kurinji country acted in haste. You yourself arrange the court.
துர்ஜயன்Kalinga, the matter has gone wrong. What we expected did not happen. Tomorrow in court our plots will be exposed. A grave will be built for this Durjayan's absolute power. My desire that my sister's son should rule this land will be destroyed. If we are to recover from this, the Maharani must die.
காளிங்கன்Yes!
துர்ஜயன்Our plan must be carried out tonight itself.
* * *
அன்பாOur plan must be carried out tonight itself.
சித்ராThen must I flee the palace?
அன்பாYes, my lady! To escape this danger, you have no choice but to leave.
சித்ராRather than leave and deepen the king's sorrow, it would be better for me to die.
அன்பாNo, Maharani! Your own life is not what matters most. The jewel growing in your womb is fit for the crown of Kurinji country. To save it, you must make this sacrifice;
சித்ராWhere can I go, Minister!
அன்பாGo through the tunnel. It will take you to the nearby village.
சித்ராMinister!
அன்பாDo not worry. As long as injustice lives in the palace, live incognito; afterward, justice itself will draw you back to the palace.
துர்ஜயன்Ah! She has escaped!!......
அன்பாCheck the pillow carefully. Kalinga, check it thoroughly............ Kalinga—ah, hahahaha!
துர்ஜயன்Laugh! Laugh well!.... Laugh out loud!... Laugh with a peal!... This is your last laugh, isn't it?... Kalinga, no one's eyes must see this fool's corpse. It must be buried in the palace garden.

சேவகன்Maharaja! We searched everywhere, but the Maharani could not be found.
கோபதிAh! She could not be found?
துர்ஜயன்Not only that, Maharaja—the minister too cannot be found!
கோபதிThe man who went searching has not returned?
துர்ஜயன்He would have to have gone searching in order to return!
கோபதிDurjaya, what are you saying?
துர்ஜயன்Is this something that even needs saying! Maharaja! When it comes to the Maharani, the minister has a special affection!
கோபதிYes! That is why he set out to search without our even telling him.
துர்ஜயன்The minister has a special ambition in the government! Even more than that, an extraordinary love for the Maharani!
கோபதிI too showed love to that demoness.
துர்ஜயன்The king's love paled by comparison; the minister's love bewitched the Maharani!
கோபதிWhat?
துர்ஜயன்His love is such that he can regard palace life and the office of minister as trifles!
கோபதிAh! An outrage impossible even to imagine!
துர்ஜயன்Yet it has happened.
கோபதிShame—great shame!
துர்ஜயன்If foreign lands hear of it, that will be an even greater shame!
கோபதிLittle foxes have plotted inside the lion's cave! Straws have played at the sword-point. Though they knew who Gopativarman was, those wicked, unjust wretches committed this outrage in his own palace. Durjaya, let the search for those traitors proceed relentlessly. Proclaim it throughout the land with drumbeat. Hack that adulteress into pieces and show her before my eyes. Wash away the stain that has fallen on Kurinji country with the blood of those wastrels. Whoever has hidden those great sinners—whoever they may be—death penalty! Torture! Hmm, let it be done.

ராணிFather!
மணிபல்லவர்My child! Look at the wonderful gift you have found for your father. Your father must give up country and city alike and go to the forest.
ராணி(She looks at the letter.) Maniballava, O king! A woman or war! Your consent is required.
Gopativarman.
Ah! What a valiant warning. Father! Write the reply—War! Write `War`.
மணிEnough—the trouble brought by your own tongue! The chick's fault has fallen upon the hen's head.
ராணிWhy are you grieving, Father? Even an ant confronts its enemy!
மணிRani! I do not want to redden the waters of the Kaveri. I have no heart to turn the warriors of Marutha country into corpses. For the peace of the country, you must marry Raudhran.
ராணிFather!
மணிCan you not make even this sacrifice for me?
ராணிFor you I would sacrifice my life. Honour is not something to be sacrificed, Father!
மணிRani! If victory is on their side, what becomes of our honour?
ராணிVictory! That is a decision the blood of warriors must determine! Why worry about it now?
மணிI know the strength of the warriors and my own strength. I do not need your preaching.
ராணிFather! Are you the one speaking like this! Born in a lineage that says spear and sword are companions in life, are you speaking like this! Born in the heroic Pandyan line that declared, `In the pale cruelty of the field of death, the shoulders that sport are our victorious shoulders`, are you speaking like this? He who blinks at the thrust of a spear is a coward! Born of the Tamil who made this his heart-song, are you speaking like this? Shame—disgrace—disgrace to the history of our country itself. The sun that has seen many battlefields will laugh! This earth that bore warriors will laugh. No, Father! Let there be no stain on the history of valour. The land will laugh that Maniballavar, afraid of death, gave his daughter as tribute to the enemy. Let that stain not fall.
மணிNo, Rani! Do not hammer my unfeeling heart with a sledgehammer. You have turned me, who wriggled like a worm, into a tiger! I will not throw what I guard like my own eye into that bear's mouth! O crowing Kurinji country! No woman for you! War—to curb your swagger!

கோபதிA very fine philosophy indeed. Let him act as he wishes.
துர்ஜயன்I do not need marriage now. Rani! Now I will see about that arrogance.

ராணிI agree. Let him go!
துர்ஜயன்We travel tomorrow—get ready! If you try to escape before then, your father's head will roll on the ground. Beware! Put this man under guard!

காண்டிபன்Where is Rani? Where? Rani! Princess Rani! Is my Rani a princess!.............
ராணிYes! A princess indeed.
காண்You said you were a servant, you masquerader! Wretch! That lust is surging in your eyes! That poison is dripping from the corners of your lips! With these, you have shattered my trust atom by atom, palace demon!
ராணிTo win your love.
காண்Love! The name of the curtain you drew over your lust-mad desire is love! The stench of rot given the name of evening-jasmine fragrance—ah! I was deceived, taking Halahala poison for nectar! I mistook sunset for sunrise and was ruined to the root! If there is such a thing as hell, I would set a snake upon your veins and have it bite you... Even then there would be no settling this deceiver's betrayal of trust. Princess—merciless demon! A Brahma-rakshasi born in a royal line that torments the poor, eats their bones, and polishes its body with them! You bewitched me with your beauty, shattered my young heart with the charm of your words, and left me stranded in the street, destroyer! The fault is not yours! Kings hunger to seize ever-new kingdoms! A king's daughter hungers to seize ever-new husbands!
ராணிI command you as princess: please leave!
காண்I am leaving, but I do not intend to go empty-handed. I will leave only after plucking out those eyes that inflamed me with desire and drove me mad! Not content with leaving the man who trusted you stranded midstream, you ordered him to leave; I will leave only after tearing out that iron heart. (He strangles her.)
ரம்பாSoldiers! Soldiers!
ராணிRun away this way.
ரௌத்What conspiracy are you plotting? Where did he come from?
ராணிHe did not come after asking me.
ரௌத்The feast meant for my sword has escaped. If that wastrel had fallen into my hands, I would have made him unrecognizable!
ராணிWarrior!
ரௌத்Doubt? Even after I made your father my slave, do you doubt my valour?
ராணிNo doubt at all! A supreme hero at dropping a stone on a sleeping man's head.
ரௌத்I did not come here to trade jokes!
ராணிNo one begged you to speak!
ரௌத்Enough of your insolence. If I leave you like this, you will escape somehow. Hey! Make preparations for the journey!.....

(Raudhran; speaks in Kandiban's voice.)
ரௌத்திரன்Rani...... Rani, come, come .. Ah! Your smile is the full moon breaking out through the clouds.
ராணிAre my lips coal-black like yours?
ரௌத்The beautiful hue of the evening sky, my dear.
ராணிWhere are you? Touch me. Won't you touch me? Don't you like me?
ரௌத்Touching you is the delight of touching dew! Would I taste fruit after tasting your cheek!
ராணிThe dawn-star of delight has risen—now onward.
ரௌத்I am the green parrot! You are the fruit-laden vine!
ராணிIn the grove of love!
ரௌத்I will not let the parrot fly away!
ராணிAh....
துர்ஜயன்The parrot. From now on, in the palace of Kurinji country, it will frolic with the prince who speaks flute-sweet words and whose eyes dart brightly!
ராணிA fine dream;
துர்ஜயன்To keep it from remaining a dream, the parrot will stay shut in a cage for a few days.
ராணிA hero only in words.
ரௌத்Watch me show my valour in action.
துர்ஜயன்Wait, Raudhra. Let her speak a little too. Is she a frightened doe or a roaring lion? Hmm, speak, girl, speak.
ராணிYou madmen who have come to think of women as objects of lust—if you are warriors, put a sword in my hand and then speak.
துர்ஜயன்We came to catch the bird with a snare, not to wound it.
ராணிA screen called clever speech for treachery—think about it. Is this human dharma: behaving so outrageously when a woman stands alone and unprotected? You too must have been born among women! Tell me—is this justice? Should you not at least respect my love, which is prepared to sacrifice country, city, mansions, all of it, for a love in accord with conviction? You have studied all the shastras! You have learned all the arts! You have turned the pages of law books! Tell me truthfully—is this dharma? Is this proper?
ரௌத்That day in the palace you put on the guise of Kali; today you shed tears and beg, you deceiver.
துர்ஜயன்Tears are women's final weapon, a shield against men. Rani, you tried hurling the Agni-astra; now you have come to the Varuna-astra. Unless you hurl the Mohana-astra, you cannot conquer the prince.
ரௌத்Why are you staring?
துர்ஜயன்She will ask Kali for mercy. That is the Kali of our realm.
ராணிI need no one's mercy. I am a Tamil woman, born in the lineage of Kannagi. My chastity will protect me.
ரௌத்We shall see whether it protects you.
காண்Let her go!

(Royal court)
கோபதிYou are guilty. Do you admit it?
காண்டிWhy ask me? That is a conclusion you must determine after an honest inquiry.
துர்ஜயன்An inquiry, Maharaja? A lamp is not needed in daylight. This case needs no inquiry at all.
கோபதிPerhaps. But in these times, when one cannot trust even one's own eyes, we must not act in haste and destroy justice. Kandiba, speak without going against your conscience. It is true that you committed the killing, is it not?
காண்டிMust I confess to murder merely for your peace of mind? Maharaja, if you possess the unswerving quality of justice, please reflect. Separation from a son heightens anguish. But one killing cannot bring peace for another.
துர்ஜயன்This is the accused's dock, not a guru's seat. You killed the prince—what is your objection to that charge?
காண்டிI have many. Men are forbidden to enter the Kali temple; what business did Durjayan and Raudhran have there at that hour?
துர்ஜயன்Lie—an utter lie. Your Majesty, ask every soldier who arrested him. Is anyone here who saw me in the Kali temple? This madman is flinging out ploughed-up arguments to save his life! The frog has thought the king's court so low!
காண்டிIs this place, which has no time to summon justice but has set aside time for this man's tormenting clamour, called a court? Is what is dispensed here called justice?
கோபதிWhat?
துர்ஜயன்This trickster says he wants divine justice.
காண்டிNo. I say human justice is enough, O king. If I am accused of killing the king's son, it is not the king who should sit here in the seat of the god of justice; it is the people of this land.
சபையோர்Yes, Maharaja. The accused's argument too is reasonable.
துர்ஜயன்Reasonable indeed. A subject kills the king himself, and then the subjects themselves deliver the verdict in the murder inquiry—that would be even more reasonable.
கோபதிYou have spoken correctly. Entering the Kali temple in violation of the law was one offence of yours.
காண்டிNo. I was not the one who broke the law. Your son was.
துர்ஜயன்There is no discussion here about the dead prince.
காண்டிThen there is no need for this inquiry at all.
சபையோர்Answer the question. There is no time for your mockery.
காண்டிHonesty appears to be mockery. Has reason gone blind because justice is in darkness? Maharaja, listen! Members of the court, listen!
துர்ஜயன்Listen! Listen! Listen carefully!
காண்Durjayan and Raudhran tried to seize my beloved. For that, at a midnight that makes thieves tremble, they entered the Kali temple. I followed. There these traitors touched and dragged my peahen-like beloved. I am poor—but does a poor man have no heart? I sprang upon these sinners who hunt maidens. This coward hid somewhere. Then came combat—sword-fight, wrestling—and from somewhere a dagger, without even my knowing it, drank Raudhran's life. That is all I know about this killing.
சபையோர்Witnesses for all this?
காண்டிNone.
துர்ஜA statement without evidence, like music without a voice, cannot come into the open, Your Majesty! Kandiban argues that Raudhran and I entered the Kali temple. Without any of the soldiers who arrested him knowing, where did I supposedly hide? Am I a magician who blinds the eyes? Or an incarnation of God? That is not the truth. Rani was in the Kali temple; Kandiban went to meet her—that was the lust of illicit love. Raudhran may have noticed it. But you may ask what that has to do with the murder. What if this man himself lured Raudhran to the Kali temple! What if Raudhran fell into his trap and this man himself killed that pure warrior!
காண்டிEven if I had killed him, it would not have been unjust. I would consider that I had completed a good deed for this country.
கோபதிWhat?
காண்Do you not know that the dead prince treated the people of this country like wild beasts?
சபையோர்This is his kingdom.
காண்Let it be the ruler's kingdom! Subjects have the right to oppose injustice. How many young women's chastity has been sacrificed to your prince's amorous diversions? How many poor people who asked `why` has your prince looked upon in their blood and laughed a ghastly laugh? Had he been left alive—had he come to the throne—would he not have made women's arms his sceptre, the harem his assembly hall, flirtatious dalliance his law book, and ruled under Rati and Madana? Would good government have prevailed in this country? By the good fortune of the people of the land, Raudhran was slain.
துர்ஜயன்Say it that way! The prince acted unjustly, and to stop that injustice you killed him!
காண்I was not granted that merit!
கோபதிThen who received it? You twisted-minded deceiver who spins barren arguments! Murderer who committed a deed that makes the heart tremble and then came here to speak of justice, fairness and integrity! Who received it, then?
துர்ஜயன்His dagger received it!
காண்The merit that did not come to me—the merit of killing our clan's treasure—came to his dagger!
(To prove that her son is innocent and that he is the prince of this country, Maharani Chitra appears there with the dagger.)
சித்ராHere is that dagger!
கோபதிWhere?
துர்ஜயன்Isn't this my dagger that went missing? How did you get it? Maharaja, some plot is under way.
கோபதிWho are you?
சித்ராDo you not recognize me? Do you not recognize this unfortunate woman, my lord?
கோபதிAh......! (In astonishment) Chitra!
துர்ஜயன்Chitra!
சபையோர்Maharani! Maharani!! Maharani!!!
சித்ராYes. The Maharani!
துர்ஜயன்The holy Maharani who went away to begin a new life with Minister Anbanandar!
கோபதிAdulteress!
சித்ராAh! My lord, kill me. Do not say that word again. To protect the child, with cruelty pursuing me wherever I went, I entrusted the diamond I had borne to an old woman and lay weeping in the Kali temple. Here—the man in the accused's dock is the prince of this country, our son.
காண்டிAm I a prince? Are you my mother?
(She shows Kandiban the medallion she had in her hand. He feels his neck and sees that she truly is his mother.)
காண்டிMother!
சித்ராDo not be troubled, my dear! From now on, good times are ours.
துர்ஜயThe king was not born into a tradition of one justice for his son and another for everyone else. Nor has he forgotten that the Maharani ran away with Minister Anbanandar.
கோபதிA son! A son! The woman who lost her reason through lust and ran away with stray dogs was supposedly lying in the Kali temple. What a tale she spins—a tale! Throw this adulteress into the underground prison. Let the law of the Kali temple that abetted this prostitute be abolished today! Take him away too. Let his head roll on the sacrificial altar today. Let the fresh blood of this sinner who killed the heir-prince soak the sacrificial altar.

(Sacrificial altar.)
துர்ஜயYour final words?
காண்டிFinal words? To whom shall I say them! To the key standing there, thirsting to drink my life? To the knife waiting to throttle my neck? Or to you, who have come to relish the sight of my flowing blood? Whoever I speak to, fine! Whoever hears, fine? At least in my final hour, let my heartbeat pound in fury and then fall still! Let my eyes blaze once and then go dark! Let my blood heat and boil, then turn cold! O rich land of noble Tamil that lived as the ground of justice and the furnace of integrity! Why have you become the dwelling of cheats, the abode of conspiracy? O golden motherland that bore only purity, love and truth! Why do you bear traitors, wicked men and villains? Is the only answer I receive to all these questions the sacrificial altar! Sacrificial altar!! Is that this terrible word? O Kurinji country where cruelty dances its tandava! Is it not enough that you kill me and grow fat by sucking my blood? Must you torment my Rani's life too? You tore my beloved away from me and locked the mother who bore me in the underground prison!
