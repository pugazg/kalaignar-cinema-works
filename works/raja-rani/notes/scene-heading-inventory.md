## Scene-separator audit

The source uses recurring **star ornamentation** to mark dramatic separation, but the exact typography varies.

Verified examples:

- PDF 10 / printed p.9: a centered row of four star ornaments separates the opening hospital sequence from the next dramatic block.
- PDF 25 / printed p.24: a centered **rule–star–rule** (`—★—`) ornament separates dramatic blocks.
- Later screenplay pages use the same separator family; OCR often misreads the ornament.
- PDF 79 / printed p.78: the location heading is star-flanked (`★ தோட்டம் ★`), and a final centered star/rule ornament closes the text.

### Important distinction

A source-visible star can perform at least two structural roles:

1. **standalone divider ornament** between dramatic blocks;
2. **decoration around a heading/location label**, such as `★ தோட்டம் ★`.

These must not be collapsed into one normalized textual form.

## Scene mapping policy

- Only printed source-supported separators/headings and clear dramatic transitions will become scene-boundary evidence.
- Page breaks alone will not create scenes.
- Story summary paragraphs will not become scenes.
- Preserve each printed ornament exactly during canonical transcription; do not replace all variants with `—★—`.
- Use rendered scan evidence, not OCR, when deciding whether a mark is a star divider, a heading decoration, or OCR noise.
- Embedded plays remain part of the parent film work and source order.
- Speaker labels will be preserved only after canonical transcription.
