## Voice decisions established by the pilot

### 1. Preserve colloquial register without caricature

The hospital exchange is informal spoken Tamil. English should remain direct and conversational rather than becoming formal medical prose. The doctor's rhetorical `சாதாரண விபத்தா அவருக்கு?` therefore remains a question, followed by the emphatic observation that survival itself is remarkable.

### 2. Retain address terms when English would over-specify them

The boy's `அம்மா!` is retained as `Amma!` because translating it as `Mother!` would create a false relationship. The doctor's familiar `...யம்மா` is likewise retained as `amma` in the later warning. Rani's final `அப்பா!...` remains `Appa!...` to preserve the one-word emotional cry.

### 3. Preserve code-switch texture

Rani's source-visible `ரொம்ப 'தேங்க்ஸ்' டாக்டர்` becomes `Many 'thanks', doctor.` The English-derived word remains visible rather than being silently replaced by a fully neutral `thank you`, because the code-switch is part of the source texture.

### 4. Preserve broken hesitation

`அப்படின்னு... அவங்கண்...?` is translated as `Then... his eyes...?`. The source's incomplete, anxious syntax is not repaired into a fully grammatical explanatory question.

### 5. Keep exact speaker labels only as source metadata

The same doctor appears under `டாக்டர்` and abbreviated `டாக்`. Translation does not normalize these labels. Every labelled utterance points to the exact immutable dialogue record that supplied it.

### 6. Stage directions remain bounded

The opening hospital direction and `[ராணி வருகிறாள்.]` are translated as two separate stage-direction units. No diagnosis, procedure, off-page movement or invented scene ending is added.
