## Scene-specific decisions

### Scene 41 — false physician and comic medicine

The scene begins in the king's palace and carries Manoharan's Akshayan disguise into Vasantha Sena's inner quarters. The source line `உமது பெயர்?` is printed without a speaker label after the letter-reading action. It is therefore represented as `manohara-en-s041-u011` with `speaker_label: null`; no speaker is manufactured from context.

The physician exchange preserves its dark comic chain rather than smoothing it out: Vasanthan complains that the physician is `taking his life`; the physician replies that this is exactly what he is trying to do; the joke then expands through Yama-Dharma, Yama's buffalo vehicle, and the old physician receiving mock condolences when the new physician arrives. Vasanthan's recurring `சந்தேகமில்லே` remains **no doubt**.

### Scene 42 — ascetic disguise, prophecy and devotional parody

The source-visible `வ. சே.` label remains exactly linked to its immutable dialogue records, including the punctuation repaired earlier from the scan. Translation does not normalize that source label.

Ugrasenan's troops enter as sadhus amid music and devotional cries. His counterfeit holy-man speech keeps **Bhagavan**, the `world is a drama` turn, **Mahalakshmi**, and **Nalayini of the Kali age** culturally explicit. Vasanthan's half-remembered devotional lesson remains elevated until he breaks it with `Vikata, I've forgotten the lesson, da...`.

The subsequent source wording `மறவு பெண்ணாசை மறக்கவேண்டும்` is translated from the booklet as printed. It is not silently corrected to a familiar devotional line and no external authorship is added. The source direction `வசந்தி போகிறான்` is rendered neutrally as **Vasanthi leaves**, avoiding an English gender insertion while preserving the named identity.

The closing action remains explicit: after the king leaves, Ugrasenan's soldiers disguised as sadhus spread through the palace, kill the guards and occupy their places.

### Scene 43 — saffron robes and the opened sluice

Manoharan's warning keeps the source's visible images: **saffron robes**, the hidden **knife-bearing army**, and soldiers pouring in **as though a sluice had been opened**. Satyaseelar's contrast between `மன வலிமை` and the enemy's `படை வலிமை` remains a contrast between strength of mind and military strength rather than being collapsed into generic strategy prose.

### Scene 44 — puja-room seduction and invasion double meanings

The `ஆண்டவன் / ஆண்டு கொண்டிருப்பவன்` turn is kept as **the Lord / the one who is ruling**. Ugrasenan's praise retains its accumulation—clouds, jasmine garden, rose field—and the source-attached gestures remain within the linked utterance.

The later exchange keeps the deliberate military/sexual double meanings: enemy troops entering the palace, reaching the inner chambers, and coming not to fight but **to surrender**. Vasantha Sena's closing **I will come tonight** is left concise rather than expanded into interpretation.

### Scene 45 — prison childbirth and reversal imagery

The corridor line `ஆள்......அப்படியா......` is not converted into a speaker label because the source prints no delimiter. It becomes `manohara-en-s045-u003` with `speaker_label: null`, while `ஆள்` remains inside the translated text as **Man...... is that so......**.

Inside the prison, Padmavathi's address to the child preserves the full contrast between a thousand nursemaids, Tamil music and a diamond-studded cradle on one side, and the straw-filled prison enclosure on the other. Vijaya's self-blame is not softened. Padmavathi's answer retains the paired reversals: **flower-feet turning earth to gold** and the woman who turned a **golden mansion into a mound of dust**.
