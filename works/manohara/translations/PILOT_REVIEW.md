## Voice decisions established by the pilot

### 1. Preserve rhetorical accumulation

The opening `வெற்றி...` speech is not compressed into a plain statement of success. Its reward/harvest imagery, repeated victory cry, clouds-of-grief image and pauses remain visible in English.

### 2. Preserve address vocabulary when it carries register

`குருதேவா`, `சுவாமி` and `குருஜி` are carried as `Gurudeva`, `Swami` and `Guruji`. Replacing all three with `teacher` or `master` would erase a source-visible shift in address and register.

### 3. Do not soften Kesari Varman's invective

The source intentionally builds a violent, theatrical catalogue against Vasantha Sena. The English retains its escalating syntax and images rather than summarizing it as anger or vengeance.

`பத்திரகாளி` remains `Bhadrakali`, and `கொவ்வை உதடுகள்` is rendered `kovvai-red lips`; these are not replaced with generic insults or generic colour language merely to sound more familiar in English.

### 4. Preserve repetition and abruptness

Short declarations such as `கிடைக்காதது கிடைத்துவிட்டது` and repeated death-denial in Kesari Varman's speech remain structurally repetitive. The translation should not remove repetition simply because polished English might prefer one sentence.

### 5. Respect page continuity

`manohara-s001-d004` crosses PDF 7→8. Its English remains the single unit `manohara-en-s001-u006`, with `english_page_segments` that mirror the physical source boundary without breaking the utterance into two speeches.

### 6. Stage directions stay source-bounded

The opening direction renders `அரூபம்` descriptively as an invisible figure but does not insert Kesari Varman's name into the direction. The later direction records only that his voice is heard and that the chisel and hammer continue working.
