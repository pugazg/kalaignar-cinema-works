## Scene-specific decisions

### Scene 11 — proclamation, parody and departure

The source-labelled `முரசறைவோன்` proclamation is represented as a `chant` unit while retaining its immutable dialogue-record link. Its repeated `போர்!`, collective commands and `டம டம டம` drum ending are preserved.

Vasanthan's mock-war speech retains the source's deliberately absurd `வைக்கோற் போர்` / `அக்கப் போர்` wordplay and its descent from tiger-eyed warriors, lions and leopards to sheep. His recurring `சந்தேகமில்லே` refrain remains `No doubt` rather than being normalized away.

The broken-sword-hilt direction genuinely crosses PDF **23→24** and remains one stage unit (`manohara-en-s011-u007`) with page-segment provenance. The later heroic-mother speech is translated as printed, including its paradoxical source wording; the English does not silently repair the anecdote.

Padmavathi's send-off preserves `பெற்ற தாயும் பிறந்த பொன்னாடும்` as the mother who bore him and the **golden native land**, while Manoharan's paired Ratna-throne / blood-soaked-sword return image remains intact.

### Scene 12 — murder bargain

The night-garden conspiracy preserves Vasantha Sena's command that Manoharan's head lie at her feet, Bauthayanan's tactical refusal to carry the head, the plan to blame the enemy, and the thousand-gold / diamonds escalation. Source voice cues such as `[ஈனக் குரலில்]` and `[மெதுவாக]` remain source-linked rather than being turned into invented character psychology.

### Scene 13 — fall of Muthu Vijayan

`பூரண கும்பம்` remains the **ceremonial full-pot** image instead of being generalized into an omen. `புகழுடம்பு பெற்றுவிட்டார்` is rendered through its classical **body of fame** image.

Muthu Vijayan's boast keeps `Blood... blood is a swimming pool to me. War is a pastime to me!`. The duel preserves the `வாய் வீச்சு / வாள் வீச்சு` tongue-swing / sword-swing exchange.

The battle, breach, death of Muthu Vijayan, victorious entry and replacement of the Pandiyan fish-flag by the tiger-flag form one continuous source action across PDF **27→28** (`manohara-en-s013-u017`).

### Scene 14 — Vijaya's vow

The minister's `அஸ்தமித்து விட்டார்` is translated as **`He has set`**, retaining the source's sunset metaphor for death. Vijaya's clipped sequence—father a corpse / flag fallen / crown fallen / vow tonight—remains an accelerating series rather than a smoothed paragraph.

### Scene 15 — Vijaya revealed

The source embeds Vijaya's involuntary `ஓ` inside stage narration; it therefore does not receive an invented dialogue record or speaker metadata.

`manohara-s015-d002` genuinely spans PDF **28→29** and remains one English dialogue unit (`manohara-en-s015-u006`). The confrontation retains the source's images: sword in bangle-bearing hands, spear-eyed women, a sword that does not bruise fruit, the Pandiyan `பைங்கொடி` as a tender creeper, and Vijaya's final skylark/vulture, freedom-breeze/slavery-cyclone and enemy-feet/death-lap parallelism.
