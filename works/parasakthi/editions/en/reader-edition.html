I created confusion in the temple—not because there should be no temple, but because a temple should not become a den of the cruel. I attacked the priest, not because he is a devotee, but to condemn devotion becoming a daytime disguise. You may ask, 'Why do you care so much—a concern no one else in the world has?' I myself was affected, directly affected! You may call it selfishness; public good is mixed into my selfishness. A fish eats filth for food and cleans the pond—like that. You call me criminal, criminal; if you walk a little way backward along this criminal's path of life, you can count how many wild rivers he has crossed. There were no singing cuckoos on my path; hooded snakes have writhed there. I have not touched the breeze, but I have crossed fire. Hear my story, Your Honour; please hear it before writing judgment! I was born in Tamil Nadu, here in Thirukkuvalai. One country to be born in, another to survive in—is my fate an exception to Tamil Nadu's fate? Rangoon nurtured my life; it raised me up. I came to see my sister in bridal adornment. Through a fraud case, here I stand before you in the accused's dock. This trickster Jolly—I too was one of those who fell into her net. I lost my cash box. I grew thin with hunger, weakened, and finally became mad. I saw the sister I had come to see—as a painting without eyes. Yes, a widow. My sister's name is Kalyani—an auspicious name! But there is no mangalyam at her neck. A flourishing family had fallen apart. A child in her hand! Tears in her eyes! Kalyani wandered; I wandered for Kalyani. Many showed Kalyani compassion. Among them were some young bulls who asked for her love. This cruel Venu, who stands before you implicated in a murder case, tried through his showiness to violate my sister. Had I not stopped him, Kalyani would have killed herself then. Devotees of God too came forward to show Kalyani compassion; in return they asked for her sidelong glance. The fiercest among them was this priest. He asked for Kalyani's chastity as an offering—in the name of Parasakthi, in the name of the Mother of the World. Kalyani might at least have remained alive, writhing like a worm in this world. It was this priest who drove her to dare suicide. She did not wish to leave her child behind in this merciless world. She did not wish to see her child die writhing without support. She herself killed it. Killing those one loves is no wonder. Gandhi, the world's noble one, embodiment of non-violence and compassion for life, is said to have told them to kill a calf writhing in disease because he could not bear to see it suffer. Kalyani used that same method. How is this a crime? A Tamil returning from abroad has no right to live; a woman born in Tamil Nadu has no suitable protection in which to live. Yet if my sister had yielded a little—if for one day in a millionaire's bedchamber she had named a price for her honour, if for one day in a mansion-dweller's lap she had carried on like this before the country—is that what this court wants? Showiness threatened my sister—she ran in fear. Money chased my sister—she ran again. Devotion frightened my sister—she ran, ran, to the very edge of life. That flight should have been stopped; her suffering should have been relieved. Did those who extend the law today do it? Did they let my Kalyani live?

[In the middle, a lawyer rises.]

வக்Accused, you are turning into a lawyer for someone else's case.

குணFor no one else's case. That too is my case—my sister's case. What is wrong in an elder brother teaching sense to the great sinner who meant to destroy his sister's honour? Kalyani attempted suicide: one crime. She killed the child: one crime. I attacked the priest: one crime. Who is responsible for all these crimes? Whose crime was it that Kalyani was left to wander without even gruel? Fate's crime, or the crime of idlers who feed their bellies by invoking fate? Whose crime was it that the money-snatching gang of robbers was allowed to grow? Famine's crime, or the crime of swindlers who bring famine to the bed? Whose crime was it that fake priests who conduct lustful games in God's name were allowed to roam the land? God's crime? Or the crime of scoundrels who conduct `kalakshepam` by uttering God's name? Until these crimes are weeded out, Gunasekarans and Kalyanis will not diminish. This is the lesson found whichever page of our book of life you turn: rationalism, useful political philosophy.

நீதிNo private individual has the right to punish an accused. Your argument must not insult the government or the law. You have been given the right only to say what happened; you must not speak unnecessarily about anything else. Answer only the questions put to you. Is Kalyani really your sister?

குணDoesn't this question seem amusing to you?

நீதிAll right. Call Kalyani.

நீதிIs he your elder brother?

கல்No. I call him `Kirukkannu`! He is not my own elder brother.

குணKalyani, I am your own elder brother! I am Gunasekaran.

நீதிWhat is it, amma?

கல்He is a little mad!

குணYes! Mad indeed. I acted like a madman so that I would not bring suffering into my sister's life. Your Honour, please don't believe this. Give me whatever punishment you want.

நீதிYou must not speak about that. That is the crime of killing the child. (Vimala enters with the child.)

விமNo! No! Kalyani committed no crime; the child was not killed either. Here is Kalyani's child.

குணVimala!

விமGuna!

நீதிHow did the child come to you?

விமOn moonlit nights I usually have the habit of going by boat on the river. At that time this child came and fell into my boat; not knowing whose child it was, I myself had been raising it. My elder brother, who had gone on tour, returned. (Flashback.)

விமLook, elder brother!

அண்Whose child is this?

விமAs usual, when I was going by boat, this child came and fell into the boat. Not knowing whose child it was, I took it and raised it.

அண்Could it be Kalyani's child?

விமWhom do you mean?

அண்Read this: a woman named Kalyani was caught when, driven by starvation, she killed her child and was about to kill herself. (Flashback.)

விமOnce I learned the truth, I picked up the child and came running.

நீதிDo you know who these people are?

விமI do! This is Gunasekaran! Kalyani is Gunasekaran's sister.

குணKalyani?
